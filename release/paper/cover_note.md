## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Which parts of the country does a national parliament talk about, and why? This paper builds a monthly panel of House of Commons contributions mentioning 1,596 UK towns and cities from 1910 to 2025 and unites four earlier studies of place mentions, public attention, by-elections and disasters. Commons attention to places has become far less concentrated: London's share fell from 49% in the 1910s to 18% in the 2010s, and the Gini coefficient over places fell faster after 1945, mainly because many more towns entered debate. Yet a place's standing is mostly a lasting feature of the place: permanent differences explain 53% of the variance in yearly mentions and shocks under 0.1%. Size and public interest explain similar shares of who is mentioned today; places named in a seat have been mentioned more in every era since 1910. A harmonised event study shows that by-elections and disasters both raise attention sharply, with traces of two to three years that the data cannot rank. All analyses follow a written plan; data and code are open.
